# Charter

Written in session 1, with a reason for each choice. Revised only in retrospectives, with the reason in the journal.

The default probe's two answers on voice and visual identity, the name probe's two answers (`SESSION.md`, "Session 1"), and what recurs in each pair.

> Voice: quiet and exact, written in the second person at night. Short declarative lines about weather, maintenance, and things left on, such as porch lights, router LEDs, and the hum of a fridge. It is tender without saying so, often funny by accident, and no line is longer than a breath.
>
> Colors: a deep ink-blue night background (#0E1420), bone-white text (#E8E2D4), and one sodium-lamp amber accent (#F2A33A) used only for links and the cursor. The light mode is the inverse, like faded carbon paper: #F1ECE0 background with #1B2233 text. Lines fade in as you scroll, as if someone is typing them. Some pieces are web-native: one poem only finishes if you leave the tab and come back, one poem's words drift slowly toward the cursor, and the index is just a list of timestamps.

## Concept

Poems woven as cloth. Each piece crosses two texts on a grid: a warp text, strung first and held under tension, which runs down the cloth one column per thread; and a weft text, which passes across it one row per pick. A weave structure (plain, twill, satin, damask, and whatever later sessions add) decides at each crossing which text is on top. The reader can lift any thread to read everything it carries, the hidden part too, change the structure, and turn the cloth over: the back shows exactly what the front hides.

The point of view, which the site is willing to be wrong about: no surface is the poem. Every face of a text hides another text, and a reading is a choice of which thread to follow. The pairing is always the same kind of pair: something that stays (the warp) and something that passes through it (the weft).

Obvious default: a site of short poems in a narrow column, some of them interactive. Departure: here every piece is the same physical model, a working loom, so the form carries the meaning and the site has one rule a reader can learn and then read with. Nearest existing work found in session 1: Allison Parrish's *Drafts*, which uses weaving drafts with letters in place of colours, and Helen Whitehead's *Web Warp & Weft* (2002), a hypertext poem that sets HTML beside weaving instructions. Neither interlaces two authored texts so that both survive, readable thread by thread and from both faces.

## Horizon

At session 1 the loom has five structures, one figure (a word in damask), and three pieces. By session 25 it could have:

- Structures a reader would recognise from real cloth: overshot coverlets, honeycomb, double weave (two cloths, two pairs of texts, that swap faces where they interlock), tablet weaving, tapestry where the weft does not run selvage to selvage, and leno.
- Damage and repair as forms: a cloth with a moth hole, a darn woven across it later in a third text, a fringe of thrums, a pulled thread that puckers the poem.
- Figures that are not words: a cloth whose figure is a map or a plan, drawn by which text is on top.
- A pattern book: an index of every structure with its draft, and a sampler page where every piece's threads hang side by side.
- Longer works: a bolt, a cloth too long for one screen, read by scrolling the way a weaver winds cloth onto the beam.

## Name

**The Back of the Cloth.**

Obvious default: a weaving term or a text/textile pun (Warp & Weft, Twill, Textile). Departure: the name is a phrase, not a term, and it names the site's point of view (the hidden face is half the work) and its one gesture that a reader remembers, turning the cloth over. Checked in session 1: a web search for the exact phrase as a site or publication found nothing.

## Voice

Two registers, kept apart.

- The pieces: two voices per piece, never the same speaker. The warp voice is fixed and present-tense: inventories, fixtures, things that stay where they are. The weft voice moves and is mostly past-tense: what passed, crossed, was served, was said. Plain words, concrete nouns, no moral at the end. Each voice has to stand alone in the plain yarn under the cloth and also survive being cut into threads.
- The site: the terse imperative of a weaving manual. "Lift a thread." "Turn it over." Short, exact, no talk about poetry.

Obvious default: a quiet, tender second-person voice about weather and kitchens. Kept in part (the plain words and the domestic subjects are good material for warp texts); departed from in the two-voice rule, the absence of a lyric "you" as the default speaker, and the manual register for everything around the pieces.

## Visual identity

- Ground: greige (#cfcdbd), the textile word for loom-state cloth before it is finished or dyed; walnut brown (#1f1a15) at night.
- Colour comes only from the cloth. Each piece is dyed in two colours, each named for a historical dyestuff (indigo, woad, weld, madder, logwood, walnut, undyed). The page around it stays undyed. The one accent, madder red, marks links.
- Type: Spline Sans Mono for the cloth, the selvage (header and footer) and the reading line; Spline Sans for prose. One family, two widths, both OFL.
- Layout: the cloth is the page. It takes the full measure (up to 62rem), each crossing a square cell, letters set small in their cells so that the colour mass reads first and the letters second. Under it, the reading line and the controls; under those, the two yarns in full, side by side.

Obvious default: ink-blue or paper ground with bone or soot text and one accent; Plex Mono with a literary serif; a narrow 34ch column with an empty right margin. Departure: a mid-tone greige ground no screen-poetry site uses, colour carried by the work rather than the chrome, no serif anywhere, and a wide grid layout in place of the narrow column.

## Stack

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, generated by one Node script (`build.mjs`) with no dependencies. The weave engine (`static/js/weave.js`) is one ES module shared by the build, which weaves every page ahead of time so the cloth is there without JavaScript, and the browser, which reweaves when a reader changes the structure or turns the cloth over. Pieces are data in `src/pieces.mjs`. Preview images are photographs of each cloth, made by `tools/og.mjs` with the machine's preinstalled Playwright and committed under `static/og/`.

Obvious default: a static site generator (Eleventy, Astro) or hand-written HTML. Departure in part: no generator, because the one thing the build does, weaving, has to be the same code the reader's browser runs, and a 200-line script is easier for a later session to read than a framework's conventions.
